The Anchor Model

By Brenden Portolese

  • org-design
  • governance
  • sdlc

AI is making execution cheap. The scarce thing is no longer the ability to write code, drafts, or analysis. It is keeping that output attached to what the organization actually intends, permits, and will stand behind.

The Anchor Model is a proposed architecture for that problem, not a proven method.

Humans own truth. AI authors its representation.

Anchors define. Orchestrators direct. Agents execute. Guardians verify. Humans decide.

Anchors are durable, human-owned representations of organizational truth: product intent, architecture, security, risk, brand, data rules. Orchestrators decide how a unit of work moves, which context applies, and when a human must step in. Agents do bounded work and are meant to be replaceable. Guardians check the result against the relevant Anchors and report drift. Humans keep ownership of truth, exceptions, and changes to the Anchors themselves.

Drift is not automatically failure. A divergence can be a mistake, a one-time exception, or evidence that the Anchor should change. That loop is the point: more machine execution without fossilizing the organization.

Software development is the first worked example. The same pattern is meant to apply anywhere AI does meaningful knowledge work.

Read in this order if you are new to it: the executive brief, then the architecture paper. Use the software and feature-spec documents when you want the SDLC version. Use the challenges page when you want the skeptical version. Appendix B of the architecture paper covers related 2026 work and what should not be claimed as distinctive.
